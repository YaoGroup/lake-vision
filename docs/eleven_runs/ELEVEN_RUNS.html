Eleven Runs for lake-vision

The ESSD baseline classifier agrees with the experts on half of the Terminal-Bench lakes, level with the frontier agents and thirty points below the humans. This is the case for ten Sherlock runs that change one thing each, and an eleventh that changes everything at once.

Where the baseline stands

The cross-year model is the one that matters: trained on the 800 CW2019 training lakes, selected on the 200 CW2019 validation lakes, tested on all 679 CW2018 lakes. It is also the only model that has never seen the 40 Terminal-Bench lakes, so it is the only one the site can honestly put on the ladder.

The confusion matrix says where the half goes. Moulin drainage is almost never called: 34 of 79 go to hydrofracture, 29 to lateral drainage. Lateral drainage leaks the other way, 87 of 253 called no-drainage. The model over-predicts ND and HF by nearly 2× against their true counts. That is a year-shift signature: 2018 was a different melt season, and a model that learned 2019's slow lateral drainages reads 2018's as lakes that never emptied.

On the 40 Terminal-Bench lakes the same model scores 63% on the 27 lakes where all three experts agree and 23% on the 13 they contest. It fails where the humans hesitate.

What the network does to a lake

Read as a pipeline, the baseline throws away information at two points, and never learns that a third kind of information is missing. The diagram is the paper's Fig. 7 with the numbers the paper did not print.

The temporal squeeze

The classifier reads only the last of 153 hidden states. Peak fill lands at day 26 to 101 in the local stacks, so the drainage the label describes must survive 50 to 130 more recurrent steps with no forget-gate bias and no normalisation. This is the standing explanation for the moulin collapse, and it has never been tested.

The spatial squeeze

Global average pooling over 32×32 turns the final map into 32 numbers. A fresh scarp, a persistent moulin or an outlet stream is a handful of cells in that map, diluted a thousandfold. The paper itself says these small transient features are what separate HF and MD from everything else.

The days that aren't there

In the local stacks 21 to 42% of days have no scene and go in as all-zero frames. Those are learnable as "blank" only approximately: in the twelve local stacks the darkest observed red pixel is 1 DN in two lakes and 1,500 to 5,300 in the other ten, and up to half a percent of real pixels sit below 100 DN, so an all-zero frame is not cleanly separable from dark ice pixel by pixel. The days that are not learnable are the partial ones, where a swath edge leaves 30 to 99% of the tile at zero and the rest real (up to 32 days a season in the western tiles), and p_water, which is NaN on 52 to 71% of days and forward-filled into a flat line that reads as a stable lake. The model has no flag for either.

And it never converged. Train macro-F1 never exceeded 0.71 in 400 epochs; the best validation epoch landed at 330 to 400 in every run, walltime-killed while still improving. At 14.5 minutes per epoch the dataloader, not the GPU, owned 99% of the wall clock. Adding capacity has been tried twice on the ESSD splits and both jobs died before their first epoch, one out of memory at 40 GB, one on a CUDA kernel mismatch. Nobody knows what a bigger model does here.

Why it ties the agents

The agents get the written protocol and the same imagery, and reason about relational cues: where the water went, whether the stream reaches the frame edge, whether a moulin sits near the basin. The network gets 32 average-pooled numbers from the end of the season plus a forward-filled area curve, from a training year with a different class mix. Parity is not surprising. The four fixes below are the cheapest ways to give the network what the agents already have.

The eleven runs

Each of R1 to R9 changes one thing against a common reference R0, so its effect is attributable. R10 stacks everything except capacity and is the ceiling estimate. All eleven submit as one SLURM array on serc A100s at the ESSD budget of 72 h each, and all run the cross-year split so the results land on the same axis as the paper and the site.

R0

Reference · the ESSD model at 32×32

main139,925 params121 GFLOP/lake

The published architecture with the accidental upsample removed and nothing else touched: RGB, p_water, last hidden state, average pool, Adam 1e-4, 400 epochs. Every other run differs from this by one change, so this run has to exist. It also answers a question on its own: did the 64×64 ConvLSTM matter? If R0 lands within noise of the paper's 0.45, the fix was free.

R1

Readout · attention over time, max beside mean over space

new ~30 lines + branch pool_type+2,100 params+0 GFLOP

Pool every hidden state, not the last one, and let a learned query decide which days matter. A single linear scores each of the 153 pooled states, softmax over time with missing days masked out, weighted sum. Spatially, concatenate max pooling to the average so a localised feature survives. This is the direct test of the temporal-squeeze hypothesis and, as a bonus, the attention weights are a per-lake curve of when the model looked, which is a figure worth having whatever the F1 does. The branch's mean and max readouts are the fallback if attention proves unstable at 153 steps.

R2

Conditioning · GroupNorm, forget-gate bias, warm-up and cosine

branch flags+240 params+0 GFLOP

Three fixes for the same disease, bundled because they answer one question: does train macro-F1 clear 0.85 once the optimisation is well conditioned? GroupNorm after each conv (not BatchNorm, the batch is 8); forget-gate bias initialised to 1.0 so the cell starts by remembering; linear warm-up then cosine decay instead of a flat 1e-4 for 400 epochs. If train rises and validation follows, the ceiling was optimisation. If train rises and validation does not, the problem is data and labels, and no architecture change will move it.

R3

Pixels · per-band standardisation, mean-fill, validity channel

new dataset code · band_stats plumbing on main+72 params+5%

The isolated test of pixel normalisation. The baseline divides reflectance by 10,000 and clips to [0, 1], a global rescale that leaves every band with its own mean and spread and every missing pixel at exactly zero. R3 standardises each band with statistics computed over the training split only, fills missing pixels at the band mean, and adds a fourth channel that is 1 where a pixel was observed. The channel is not a separate idea: once missing pixels are mean-filled they become invisible, and without it a half-tile swath edge is indistinguishable from real ice, while with zero-fill the same pixels would standardise to about −7σ and dominate every conv they touch. Standardise-and-flag is one change. The August audit rated the input representation the highest-leverage axis on the board; the band-statistics plumbing exists on main but has never been fed a file.

R4

Observed flag · tell the scalar stream where the gaps are

main use_cloudyseq + a new flag series+2,240 params+0 GFLOP

The scalar side, isolated. p_water is NaN on 52 to 71% of days and forward-filled, so more than half of every area curve is a copy of the last good value, and a flat line is exactly what no-drainage looks like. Nothing in the pixels can reveal this. R4 feeds a second [153 × 1] series through the existing cloudy_seq slot and its own small LSTM: 1 where p_water was measured that day, 0 where it was filled. The code path exists on main; only the flag series is new, and it comes straight from the deposit file.

R5

Masks · the static outline and the daily water mask as channels

branch mask_as_channel, extended+140 params+10%

The published run loaded the static lake boundary and discarded it; the dynamic NDWI mask in the deposit has never been used. Feed both as channels. The outline is genuine outside information, a maximum extent the model cannot derive from one season. The daily mask is derived from the same pixels, so it adds no information, but it hands the first conv layer the one thing every class definition is written in terms of: where water was, and where it no longer is. The difference between the two channels on any day is a drained-fraction map. Whether water left through the outline's edge or vanished inside it is the LD-versus-HF question drawn as an image.

R6

Attention · spatial CBAM on the feature map

main attention_type=spatial+100 params+0 GFLOP

The learned spatial attention already in the repo, never run on the ESSD splits. It re-weights the 32×32 map before the ConvLSTM so an outlet stream or a crevasse field can be emphasised without being averaged away. This is the attention mechanism that suits the protocol; the repo's other option, arch, multiplies the features by a CNN of the mask, which zeroes everything outside the lake and is exactly the wrong inductive bias for a taxonomy whose decisive cues (outlet streams, moulins, crevasse fields) sit outside the basin. Dynamic masks and attention combine in R10 by giving CBAM the mask channels of R5 to look at, not by gating on them.

R7

Spectral · near-infrared and SWIR 1610 nm

use_nir and use_swir16 on main; the dataset already selects deposit bands by name+140 params+10%

Water absorbs strongly in the near and shortwave infrared; slush, refrozen lids and shadowed ice do not. The bands are in every deposit file and have never been consumed. The August notes called SWIR16 the most promising unexplored lever for moulin drainage, where a lake that drains slowly through a persistent hole looks, in RGB, much like one refreezing in place.

R8

Soft targets · train on the labelers' probabilities

new loss, ~15 lines+0 params+0 GFLOP

Every lake in the deposit carries a five-way probability vector, not just an argmax. Cross-entropy against that vector, with the same inverse-frequency class weights, stops punishing the model for hedging exactly where the labeler hedged. The 40-lake result is the motivation: 23% on the contested lakes against 63% on the unanimous ones. A model trained on hard labels learns that a 0.5/0.5 MD/LD lake is definitely one of them.

R9

Capacity · 16 base channels, 64 hidden

main flags + gradient checkpointing546,789 params3.7× GFLOP

The run that died twice, resubmitted with the 32×32 map on an 80 GB A100, with the SKU constraint the second attempt lacked. The diagnosis says this is the least likely lever: the current model underfits, and the one earlier high-capacity run on the combined split peaked at 0.57 by epoch 49 and then overfit. It is here because the question keeps coming back and a clean null on the cross-year split closes it. It is the only run whose cost is not free, and the last to be cut if the budget shrinks.

R10

The stack · R1 to R8 together

everything above except R9144,976 params150 GFLOP/lake, 1.24× R0

Eight channels in (RGB, NIR, SWIR16, validity, outline, daily mask), standardised; GroupNorm; spatial CBAM; ConvLSTM with forget bias; attention over time and avg + max over space; the area curve and its observed flag; soft targets; warm-up and cosine. This is the best guess at the ceiling, and the run whose number goes on the site if it clears the others. R3 and R4 travel together here, which is fine: the stack is the ceiling, not the attribution. It is also the one that most deserves a second seed, because ±0.03 to 0.05 macro-F1 is single-split noise on a 200-lake validation set.

The stack, drawn

Protocol

  • Split. The committed cross-year split, unchanged: train 800 and val 200 from CW2019, test all 679 of CW2018. Headline numbers stay comparable to the paper and the deposit.
  • Selection. Best validation macro-F1 epoch, as in ESSD. Test is scored once per run, at the end. Also score the 40 Terminal-Bench lakes, since that is the bar the site shows.
  • Noise. A 200-lake, five-class validation set carries ±0.03 to 0.05 macro-F1. Treat anything smaller as a tie; promote a change only if it clears that on val and does not fall on test.
  • The gate. Log train macro-F1 every epoch. The question the array answers first is whether any run gets train above 0.85. That decides whether the next round is architecture or data.
  • Budget. Eleven runs at 72 h on serc A100s, submitted as one array. Ten of the eleven cost within a quarter of the reference. If the colleague's cached pipeline has landed, epochs drop from 14 minutes to minutes and the same array finishes in a day.

After the results

  • If R10 beats R0 by more than noise and one or two singles carry most of it, rerun those singles with a second seed and put the winning single on the site.
  • If R2 lifts train F1 but not val, stop tuning: the next lever is the 5,000-lake expansion and label quality, not runs.
  • If R3 moves the number and R4 does not, or the reverse, the input story is told in one line: pixels or gaps. If both move it, the stack owes most of its gain to the cheapest changes on the list.
  • If R1 lifts moulin drainage specifically, the transformer question is closed. If it does not, a temporal transformer over per-frame pooled features is finally worth a run, and the August notes already scope it.
  • If R9 does nothing again, that is the third null and the capacity question is retired for JSTARS.

Not proposed, and why

  • A vision transformer. A model that cannot fit its own training set does not need more capacity and less inductive bias. The thing a transformer would fix, the last-step readout, is R1 at zero cost.
  • Mask-gated features (arch attention). Zeroes the context outside the basin, which is where the protocol's decisive cues live.
  • Downscaling or subsampling the season. Ruled out earlier; every memory problem here has a fix that keeps the data.
  • The cloud mask as an input. Measured in August: water is 0.16× as likely to be detected under it, and its zero means either clear or unobserved. The validity channel carries what it should have.
  • Changing the split or the year. The cross-year number is the one the paper, the deposit and the site all quote. Cross-validation on the combined pool is the right second step for JSTARS, not the first.

The gap the proposal glosses over

Main's LakeDataset reads the deposit's reflectance and p_water (it parses band_name), but its only notion of a mask is a channel inside the reflectance array, which the legacy composites had and the deposit does not. The deposit stores lake_boundary [y, x] and water_mask_ndwi [time, y, x] as separate variables, and the shelved branch only handled them in its cache path, which was rolled back. So R3, R4 and R5 need _load_from_disk taught the deposit layout: read the two mask variables, broadcast the outline over time, append both as trailing channels, and derive the p_water observed flag from the NaNs before the ffill/bfill. That is the first piece of code to write, and it is the one that needs a test against a real deposit file.

Specifications for the new pieces

  • Attention readout (R1). After the ConvLSTM, pool every hidden state spatially (avg, or avg‖max when pool_type=both) to [B, T, D]. Score with one Linear(D, 1), mask days whose validity flag is 0 to −1e4, softmax over T, weighted sum. Save the weights per lake at inference. Flag: --temporal_readout attn beside the branch's last/mean/max.
  • Validity channel (R3). Per pixel, per day: 1 where the red band was finite, 0 where NaN. Appended after the reflectance channels, before any mask channels. Missing pixels are then filled at the training-split band mean, not zero.
  • Band statistics (R3). A script that streams the 800 training files, accumulates per-band mean and std over finite pixels only, and writes band_stats.json in the format load_band_stats already expects. Never compute over val or test.
  • Observed flag (R4). [T, 1], 1 where p_water was finite in the file, 0 where it was filled. Fed through --use_cloudyseq --cloudy_seq_var observed; the ScalarLSTM path already exists. Do not confuse it with the legacy cloudy_seq_rgb (1 = useful) or eo_cloud_cover (1 = cloudy, scene-level).
  • Masks as channels (R5). Both variables as trailing channels, outline broadcast over T. Feeds FrontCNN directly; attention_type stays none for R5 and spatial for R10, never arch.
  • SWIR16 (R7). Index the reflectance array by band_name, never by position; the deposit order is red, green, blue, nir, swir16, swir22. Assert the channel count after loading.
  • Soft targets (R8). −Σ_c w_c · p_c · log softmax(z)_c with the same inverse-frequency w_c, p from the label CSV's five probability columns. Hard-label metrics unchanged.
  • Schedule (R2). Linear warm-up over 10 epochs to 1e-4, cosine to 1e-6 at epoch 400. GroupNorm with 8 groups clamped to the channel count. Forget-gate bias 1.0 on the ConvLSTM cell only.
  • Every checkpoint carries {state_dict, config, git_sha, run_id}, as the branch's provenance dict does. Bare state dicts are how the last capacity run became unidentifiable.

Memory, measured

Both capacity runs died for reasons that are now known and neither was the model's fault alone. Job 22043892 ran out of memory inside the front CNN's max-pool at 35 GB on a 40 GB A100, with bf16 autocast already on. Job 22429909 asked for GPU_MEM:80GB instead of the A100 SKU, landed on an H100, and the py-pytorch/2.2.1 module has no sm_90 kernels for cuDNN's LSTM, so it died in flatten_parameters before the first batch. Every script in the array must carry -C GPU_SKU:A100_SXM4.

The table is mem_bench.py: the bytes each run saves for backward, counted with saved-tensor hooks on the repo's own modules under bf16 autocast at batch 1, scaled to batch 8 × 153 frames, plus the fp32 input batch. The front CNN is the whole footprint. Its first conv layer at 512×512, the LeakyReLU output and the int64 max-pool indices dominate, which is why main's whole-FrontCNN checkpoint recovers only 1 to 2 GB: the checkpointed segment rematerialises all four layers at once. GroupNorm adds ten GB because it saves the conv output a second time.

Either way pass --host_mem_budget_gb 200: at 8 input channels a sample is 1.3 GB and 12 workers × 2 prefetch × batch 8 is 246 GB, right at the template's 256 GB and enough to get R10 killed by the cgroup rather than the GPU. The three big runs should also ask for --mem 320GB, as the earlier high-capacity scripts did.

Wall clock is the other hard limit. At 14.5 minutes an epoch the healthy runs projected 100 h for 400 epochs and were killed at 72 h; the best-F1 checkpoint is written as it improves, so a killed run still leaves a usable model, but it does not leave a clean stop. Either accept about 290 epochs or pass --epochs 290 so every run ends the same way. Log torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated() once per epoch; the smoke job reads it against this table.

Full runs, 2026-09-09: R1 is unstable, two follow-ups added

Submitted by Josh at 96 h (jobs 42611963, 42611969, 42611971). Epochs run 7 to 10 min, not the 12 to 28 the smoke projected, so wall clock is comfortable. By epoch 110 R0, R3, R4 and R2 train smoothly (R2 leads on train F1 at 0.59, R3 on val at 0.56), but R1 collapses and recovers repeatedly: train loss jumps at epoch 60, val loss spikes at 67, train F1 falls from 0.50 to 0.22, recovers by 100, collapses again at 111. Its periodic snapshots, run on the twelve local lakes, show the attention is not the culprit: entropy stays at 4.6 to 4.7 nats against 5.03 for uniform and no day ever gets more than 2% of the weight, so the readout is effectively a mean. The one thing that changes from R0 is that the loss gradient now enters the ConvLSTM at all 153 steps instead of only the last, through an unnormalised recurrence with an unbounded cell state and no gradient clipping. The collapse-then-slow-recovery shape is what a gradient spike does under Adam (the second-moment estimate stays inflated for about a thousand steps, ten epochs here).

Two 40 GB follow-ups, in eleven_runs_matrix.sh as EXTRA_RUNS and array indices 11 and 12: R11 --temporal_readout mean (isolates the readout from the avg‖max pooling and the scorer) and R12 = R1 + --grad_clip 1.0. The trainer now logs the pre-clip global gradient norm (mean and max over the epoch's steps) on every epoch line, for every run, so R12 measures the diagnosis instead of assuming it: norms that spike where R1 collapsed close the case. R1 keeps running; the R1/R12 pair is the experiment.

  • All eleven configurations run 2 epochs on the twelve local stacks on the Mac (CPU, batch 1: the Mac has 17 GB) and produce a finite loss. This catches shape and flag errors in minutes.
  • Then one Sherlock smoke job, with Josh's OK: the array over 50 lakes for 5 epochs. Both earlier capacity runs died before epoch 1; this is the check that would have caught them. Record epoch time and peak GPU memory per run here, against the 72 h budget and the memory table.
